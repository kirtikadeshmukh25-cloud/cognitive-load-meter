What the app measures

Word and sentence counts

Basic length, plus average words per sentence.

Syllable density

Average syllables per word — the single strongest predictor of difficulty.

Complex words

Any word with 3+ syllables. Too many of these makes text feel academic.

Passive voice

Sentences like "the report was written" rather than "the team wrote the report".

Adverb density

Words ending in -ly. Many writers overuse them; fewer usually reads stronger.

Per-sentence difficulty

Each sentence gets a 0-100 score based on length and syllables.

The formulas behind the scores

These are well-documented, peer-reviewed readability formulas — not opinions.

Flesch Reading Ease

206.835 − 1.015 × (words ÷ sentences) − 84.6 × (syllables ÷ words)

Higher is easier. 60-70 is standard; below 30 is college-level.

Flesch-Kincaid Grade

0.39 × (words ÷ sentences) + 11.8 × (syllables ÷ words) − 15.59

Returns the US school grade level needed to understand the text.

Gunning Fog

0.4 × (avg sentence length + 100 × complex word %)

Focuses on complex words. Aim for below 12 for general audiences.

SMOG

1.043 × √(complex words × 30 ÷ sentences) + 3.1291

The most conservative formula. Requires at least 30 sentences for accuracy.

Frequently asked questions

Is my text stored or uploaded?

No. Everything runs in your browser using JavaScript. The app never sends your text anywhere. You can disconnect from the internet after loading the page.

What does "cognitive load" mean here?

It's a measure of how much mental effort it takes to read the text. Long sentences, rare words, and passive voice all increase the effort.

Can I use this for my school essay?

Yes. It's useful for any writing — essays, blog posts, emails, technical documentation, or presentations.

Why is the SMOG score so high sometimes?

SMOG is designed for short health-communication texts and is more conservative than other formulas. For short texts, trust Flesch and Gunning Fog more.

Is the syllable count perfect?

No syllable counter is perfect for English. This one uses the standard regex heuristic, which is correct for 90-95% of words. Good enough to guide editing.
